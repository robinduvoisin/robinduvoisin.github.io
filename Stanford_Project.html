Minimally Invasive Kirigami-Inspired Microelectrode Array for Deep Brain Recording

Supervisor
Prof. Todd Coleman, Prof. Zhenan Bao and Tomasz Zaluska
Institution
Stanford University

Abstract

The ability to record neural activity directly from the human thalamic circuit will have major impact in advancing neuroscience, medical research, and brain-machine interface technologies. Yet, all currently available microelectrode arrays (MEAs) rely on the insertion of multiple electrodes from different sites, resulting in highly invasive procedures. In this report, we address this challenge by presenting a minimally invasive kirigami-shaped microelectrode array. Designed to expand after insertion, the device can be deployed against the wall of the third ventricle. To achieve this breakthrough, we extended the theoretical framework of bistable kirigami to engineer geometries that meet the strict constraints of neurosurgical application. Specifically, we introduced a novel bistable rotating-unit kirigami design with a zero Poisson ratio and establish a complete pipeline for optimizing its structure for flexible biocompatible materials.
