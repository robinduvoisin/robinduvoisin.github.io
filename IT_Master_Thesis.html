Energy-Efficient Neural Codes: Comparing Receptive-Field and ON–OFF Encoders

Supervisor
Prof. Jean-Pascal Pfister, Dr. Pau Vilimelis Aceituno
Institution
University of Bern

Abstract

In sensory neural systems, tuning curves define the relationship between an external stimulus and a neuron’s firing rate. Across species and sensory modalities, two archetypal tuning curve profiles are commonly observed: ON–OFF encoders, in which neurons signal increases or decreases in stimulus in- tensity, and receptive-field encoders, in which neurons respond selectively to restricted regions of the stimulus space. However, the computational principles that determine when one strategy should be fa- vored over the other remain unclear. In this report, we investigate these two encoding paradigms through the lens of energy-efficient neural coding, where the goal is to maximize information transmission per unit of metabolic cost. We first establish a mathematical equivalence between ON–OFF encoders and non-overlapping receptive-field encoders. We then show that receptive-field encoders can outperform ON–OFF architectures in terms of capacity per unit energy. Finally, we discuss how these results can motivate a biologically plausible adaptive mechanism for maintaining energy-efficient communication in variable environments.
